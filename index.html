50 Ways of Seeing: Face Iteration

0 / 0

Description of My Work

I used my own face as a living canvas for this assignment. I translated the familiar shapes of my features into fifty visual reverberations. I created photographic collages, and abstract/surreal physical and digital compositions. To build/construct a layered self, I gathered what I had on hand (cardboard and foam packing materials, printer paper, photographic prints, tape, glue, and scrap fabric) and got to work. What came about is a pageless book constructed in the exact silhouette of my face and its side profile. Much in line with the societal impulse to use the face as a "text" (full of emotions, character, shapes and colors) to be "read", I materialize this concept and framework for reading the self. The iterations, glued in various overlapping ways, are meant to have varied interpretations and experiences by the viewer of where one iteration starts and another ends. Utilising my experience with body dysmorphia, I attempted to translate that experience to the physical, material page, through this subjective lens.



Reflection


What changed as you progressed?
As I brainstormed and progressed through the creation of fifty different ways of seeing or iterating a reverberation of my face (whether that be my face shape, structure, composition, colors, feel, etc.), I began to challenge myself to step away from the real and experiment with abstraction. I started with a simple sketch of my face. Pencil, paper, and a visual that makes sense (one can see it in the everyday) and is recognizeable. As I moved through different physical (cardboard, foam) and digital materials (using photopea) and edits, I tried to break down the shapes of my face using the tools at hand to provide an experimental series of iterations that still held the essence of my face or side profile but maybe perhaps were less on the nose and more investigative in their presence. For instance, I started chopping up visuals and layering them to create something new.

When did you run out of obvious ideas?
When editing with the digital and photopea, I found myself a little distanced from the physicality of my face. So, I tried bringing back the tangibility/touchable aspect. I pushed a foam sheet onto my face and started to fill in the intrusions (eye sockets) and extrusions (nose) with ink.

Which materials changed how you thought about the object?
When I started working with fabric, the whole piece started coming together. I sourced scrap pieces of fabric and ended up creating a book that opens wide to reveal my fifty iterations. The fabric tied (pun intended) my piece together, literally and metaphorically. It was also nice to work with materials that have already had a lived existence, or previous life of their own. In that way, I had the opportunity to reinvent these once storied materials (cardboard and foam wrap for packaging during my move to Brooklyn, scrap fabric) into something new.,

What surprised you?
I was surprised by the end product. It was not something I anticipated or planned upon but that instead came together through many a night of creativity and exploring my own self expression and view of myself.

Which experiments would you want to continue?
The exploration of how I see and display myself. As someone who has experienced body dysmorphia throughout my younger years, it is interesting to see how my self concept has evolved.

Endangered Species: Kemp's Ridley Sea Turtle

0 / 0

Description of My Work

I took a species I am very familiar with, turtles, and decided on one that is native to our East Coast of the United States to develop into a sculpture: the Kemp's Ridley Sea Turtle.



Reflection


In your process documentation, identify two main takeaways from the project. These might address something you learned about your species, something you discovered about waste or materials, a connection you began to see between the two, or something the making process caused you to think about differently.


Two main takeaways:

(1) From my research, I have learned that the Kemp's Ridley Sea Turtle is not only the smallest sea turtle in the world but also the most endangered due to climate change, oil spillage impacting its environment and migration patterns, fisheries and netting (getting caught by fisherman by accident), and pollution (plastic waste that the turtles consume, get caught in, or injured by). I selected this sea turtle as it is native to our East Coast and New York City, close to home. I found that these turtles uniquely have heart shaped spherical shells, with an almost heptagonal line down the caraface (the highest/tallest part of the shell) and center of the back of the shell.

(2) I discovered that I could pleat cardboard (in an effort to crimp the nose to make a recognizeable essence of the Kemp's ridley sea turtle)! I also discovered that I could peel back one length of wall on a piece of cardboard and retain an almost fully intact ribbed inner side of the cardboard to create texture on my endangered species sculpture.

Additional takeaways:

- In terms of a connection between the materials I used in making my Kemp's Ridley Sea Turtle sculpture, I sourced cardboard boxes, hot glue (and hot glue gun), plastic bottles, plastic caps, and mesh plastic netting. In doing so, I highlighted several of the factors endangering the lives of this turtle species including fisheries through use of the netting (which I also folded the mesh to also represent waves of water for environment) wrapped around the body of the sea turtle sculpture, I inserted plastic bottles into the body of the turtle's shell to represent pollution (the ingestion of and interference by), the cardboard symbolizing the oil spills that can drench dumped cardboard (once-packaging or materials) that sea turtles then mistaken for food which can poison turtle populations (also worthy of noting: debris like cardboard and plastic waste have a tendency to drift alongside oil spills due to ocean currents).

- The making process caused me to think about my personal connection to rescuing both sea and land (truly lake or pond) turtles in a different physical way. How could I materialize my experience whilst responding to the bounds of this assigned project? Growing up, I helped sea turtle hatchlings reach the sea (and kept the birds and onwatchers at bay), along with saving and rehabilitating turtles that had gotten hit by cars (which cracked their shells) or even freeing an injured turtle from having fallen in a nearby sewer drain. Taking my personal experience, I began to shape the body and especially the back shell of the sea turtle with my background in mind. I left gaps in the caraface (upper most points) of the shell, to symbolize these gaps (both cracks in the shell literally, and a broken body metaphorically from pollution and other threats).

Playful Interfaces

Reflection



3 Tensions: Vibrancy/Brightness vs. Dullness, Active seeking out vs. Passively receiving content/information, Alertness/Availability/Accessibility Always




What became possible through the physical interface that was not possible (or not emphasized) in the original?

The physical wearable interface allowed for the absurdity of our artificial rules/expectations on social media to be put on full display. It also removed the invisibility cloak over the act of trapped waiting to receive a notification, by bringing attention to the availability to receive human connection through the flag and the open (no clapse to close) mailbox. It becomes visible. In this way, it visualizes this hidden tension of social media containing bright saturated content but its actual operation having the opposite impact (consuming it, especially in large amounts, can make a human feel hollow, lost, or disconnected from oneself).


How did the physical qualities of your materials, body, or environment shape your interaction design?

I ended up making a fabric sash (made in a quilt like manner with scrap fabric), a mailbox that ties with fabric around a user's waist, and a bandana an individual can wrap around their forehead (that has a moveable mailbox flag). The fabric is colorful whereas the cardboard mailbox and flag are dull. In doing so, the vibrancy of the scrap fabric placed an emphasis on the colorfulness of human stories, warmth, comfort even. The cardboard acts in opposition to this image, dull and limiting (mechanical almost) providing an apt commentary on how social media truly is.
I tied the mailbox around the waist at the point of center of gravity for an individual to highlight the act of receiving impacting someone's balance (and then allow reflection upon the impact of receiving information on the mind and body from socials). Putting the mailbox flag on my head speaks to this as well, that someone may not be open to receiving but may receive anyway (and vice versa).


What surprised you when someone actually tried it?

When a friend of mine did a test run with me and interacted with my wearable sculpture art, I found it shocking how vulnerable and invasive the exchange felt. Intimate almost. On my cellphone, I control the so-called "barrier" or distance in line with my comfortability levels. And at the end of the day, I am looking through a glass screen at a phone and not interacting with individuals face to face. Letting people around me interact with my human mailbox or receiver (as a metaphor for what social media really is), I experienced a truly surprising feeling of this vulnerability and closeness (not always a good thing, at times shocking and unwelcome even when I know the person).


If you continued developing the interface, what would you change?

I would add signage for directions on what to fill the mailbox with. In doing so, it would allow for me to walk along the streets of NYC and see what random passerby might slip into my mailbox.



GenAI as Creative Material: Timothy H. O'Sullivan's (c. 1840–1882) Worker, Gould & Curry Mine, Nevada

0 / 0

Reflection








Selected Artist and Work: Timothy H. O'Sullivan's (c. 1840–1882) Worker, Gould & Curry Mine, Virginia City, Nevada (also archived as Interior of Virginia City, Nevada Mine, 800 Feet Below)


When and where were they working?

O'Sullivan photographed this picture in early 1868 within the Comstock Lode (the first big discovery of silver ore in the United States) in Virginia City, Nevada.

What were the fashions, aesthetics, politics of the region they produced this work in?

Politics:
This work was produced at a booming time for Virginia City, Nevada due to the discovery of an impressive amount of silver (called the Comstock Lode). Virginia City went from being an isolated territory to becoming the epicenter of the Comstock Lode, causing it to gain prominence during and after the American Civil War. The wealth that came of this discovery directly contributed to funding the Union in the Civil War and accelerating Nevada's journey toward's statehood.

Aesthetics:
Mining for silver required bigger corporations, heavy machinery and complicated wooden beams and supports to prevent tunnels from collapsing or caving in while exploring far underground.

Fashion:
In this dangerous and health-costly environment, miners wore rough and practical outdoors-wear like thick denim pants, thick wool shirts, and protective head gear to survive during their working hours.

What mediums or materials did they use?

American photographer and journalist, Timothy O'Sullivan, made use of wet-plate collodion (an early photographic technique combining a fluid base to bind light-sensitive silver salts to glass or metal) on glass negatives, a transportable darkroom kit, incredibly explosive magnesium wire flares used for light, and albumen paper (which was treated with egg whites) for the final photographic prints.

How was the work received in its time?

In 1868 and the near years that followed, this photograph was used as a scientific tool or reference. Although this photo was taken as a means of scientific record for the King Survey Reports and acted as a means of raw data and evidence of the conditions of the Nevada mines, it may have been ultimately omitted from the final report due to being deemed an oddity (as many of O'Sullivan's works were, because of the composition - as many photos were missing aspects due to dark print spots and the lack of light in the mines).

How is the work perceived today?


Today, this photograph almost stands as fine art/ photographic material in a historic industrial realm. It speaks to the history of human labor, especially in the United States (for anyone interested in studying it) and is appreciated for its high contrast rather than criticized for it.

What I find compelling, interesting, creepy, fascinating, unsettling, groundbreaking, compositionally challenging, and psychologically charged about the work I have selected is the symbolism of a portal or doorway to nowhere. I have personally explored supposed "deadends" and the piece incredibly relates to my interest in collective resilience despite the odds.

What makes the work unique:
 Composition: The solo miner stands out in the right fore/midground, distinctly facing and against a sizeable, empty cave wall space.
Lighting: This speaks most to the tools used in the making of this artifact. The quick, blinding blast of light from the magnesium flash on O'Sullivan's camera illuminates the miner's back and tool, the wall, and bench, leaving the rest of the tunnel in an unsettling darkness.
Lines and Angles: The photograph has sharp, high contrast, organic lines. The angle of the miner's tool, his bent back, and the jagged rocks almost cause a feeling of heavy air and hard work.
Scale and Size: The huge, heavy layers of rock completely overwhelm the size of the single worker, showing just how small and fragile a human can look deep inside the earth.
Atmosphere: The feeling is one of incredibly heaviness, quiet, and crampedness. As an observer, one can almost sense the dusty air and the sudden flash of light cutting through a deep darkness.
Historical connection: The photograph captures a pivotal moment when the so-called "wild" American West transitioned from an image of open nature into a commonplace of massive, corporate run mining initiatives.
Description from my text prompt:
 A high-contrast, sepia-toned old photograph made on a glass plate. In the middle of the picture, a single, strong male mine worker stands hundreds of feet underground. We see him from a three-quarters back angle, wearing rough 1800s work clothes: tough denim pants, a thick flannel shirt with rolled-up sleeves, and a simple work hat. The miner is caught in mid-action, straining hard as he swings a hand-pick into a jagged wall of silver rock that fills the right side of the picture. The entire scene is lit up by a single, harsh flash of artificial light from the left. This bright light shines brilliantly across the worker's back, his tool, and the rough texture of the stone. The rest of the picture is completely pitch-black, creating a spooky and cramped feeling where the brightly lit man is surrounded by a deep, dark void that swallows the top and left edges of the photo. The final image looks old and dusty, with deep charcoal shadows and warm brown tones.


Analyze your results. Where did the models diverge from your prompts? What did they “get right” based on your expectations of the final result?



While the models achieved the sepia coloring, the high contrast, and old photograph ambience of the original photograph, its contents differed. One miner remained, facing right on all 3 recreations, and most in the right fore/midground of the composition of the page. However, all of the recreated iterations had a standing miner, while in the original the man was sitting. Just as well, the three recreations showed more force than the original. Perplexity got the orientation of the arms and hands wrong to human anatomy. Chatgpt forewent the wheel. Gemini included the wheel but dramatically increased its size and placed the worker in the center of the page inaccurately.



Two Buttons / Daily Struggle Superman Meme.



Collage this meme with the image: A close-up shot of a panicked superhero wearing a red bodysuit and gloves, agonizing in front of two massive, glowing red buttons. The superhero is sweating profusely, with thick beads of sweat dripping down his face and a strained, wide-eyed expression of sheer panic as he faces an impossible dilemma. The two large red buttons sit on a control panel directly in front of him.


All three images made a real man out of the original meme, and most almost forgot the original contents of my initial image. Perplexity got the ambience of the original description right, with the old photograph and sepia look. The other two, Gemini and ChatGPT looked much more cinematic and modern in contrast. All showed more men behind the subject. The miner is gone in all of them. The superhero is panicked, as is the meme, provided two buttons to decide between.

For my next iteration, I provided the prompt "bring closer to the original, make the subject a miner". Perplexity I learned only supplies one image at a time, so I signed into different accounts for each run of commands asking for a visual.


All three reverted back to a closer rendition of the original image, seemingly struggling to create a collage of both.

For my next iteration, I provided the prompt "make into a sketch, collaged with the indecision of the superhero between the two buttons. make the superhero and miner the same character".
Analyze your results:


How close did each image AI model adhere to your prompt?

None of the models adhered so well to my prompts consistently. However, Gemini and Perplexity at times were a bit more in tune than Chatgpt (as seen by the lack of following instructions).

Was there anything that frustrated, perplexed, angered or bored you about each generated image? Bias? Slop? Hallucinations? 6 fingered hands?

At first I was a bit disturbed and dissappointed by the lack of following directions. AI really struggled with the idea of a collage.

Was there anything that surprised, impressed or humored you?

Yes! I was not expecting the crowd, at times related to bikers, or the human-like superhero miner figure.

Did you feel AI surpassed your expectations in any way?

Not really. As an artist, one who started in photography, film and fine art, I can see how AI can be used as a tool to expedite certain highly utilised functions. However, when it comes to anything irregular, AI appears to have difficulty at times.

At this point, would you call this work “your own”?

No.

GEN AI SUMMARY

What did the systems seem to understand?
The AI systems seemed to have understood the broad ideas and qualities of my original prompt (sepia coloring, high contrast, older photographic appearance/texture, a miner facing towards the right, and a dark underground as the backdrop). They also understood parts of the meme, especially the panicked superhero and the two buttons. What was understood way less consistently was the relationship between these visuals and then the collage ask I made of them. I wanted a collage connecting labor and indecision, rather than one subject replacing another. What did they ignore?
They systems often ignored the original photograph’s composition (the miner’s less forceful posture and position). However, one noticeable discrepancy came from my own prompt: I accidentally described a standing miner swinging a pick, even though my reflection explains that the original miner was seated. In that situation, the systems followed my instructions rather than the photograph I intended to recreate. Later, when I introduced the meme, the systems dropped the miner entirely, and when I prompted them to restore the miner, they struggled to preserve the meme. What did they add that you never asked for?
They added an exaggerated/dramatic physical effort and staging/position, and a more cinematic/high contrast atmosphere. Gemini enlarged the wheel, while Perplexity rendered an anatomically incorrect arrangement of arms and hands. The background crowds also became a prominent feature, that I did not expect. I had not intended the surrounding figures to compete with the central subject. What seems easy for the tools? What seems difficult?
Applying an overall style seemed relatively easy: old photograph, sepia, high contrast, cinematic realism, or sketch. Recognizable subjects and emotions also came through quickly. What seemed difficult was maintaining several relationships at once: the original composition, the miner’s identity, the superhero’s indecision, the two buttons, and a collage structure. The tools could produce individual ingredients more reliably than they could preserve my intended connection between them. Where do you see clichés, conventions, artifacts, or “AI-ness”?
I saw AI-ness in the exaggerated muscular labor, theatrical panic, cinematic lighting, and polished historical atmosphere. These choices made the images immediately readable but less specific to what interested me in O’Sullivan’s photograph. The incorrect anatomy was an obvious artifact. More subtly, the images often replaced the original’s unsettling stillness with a familiar dramatic scene. What kinds of creative decisions were you making versus what decisions was the system making for you?
I chose the source photograph, the meme, the conceptual connection, and the sequence of revisions. I wanted to connect the miner’s physical struggle with the psychological struggle of making a decision. The systems determined much of the actual execution: pose, framing, proportions, lighting, facial expression, and how the two sources would be combined—or whether one would disappear. I directed the concept, but I did not consistently control the visual relationships that gave that concept meaning.


GEN AI MODELS ANALYSIS

Which tool gave you the most control?
None gave me dependable control throughout the process. Based on my observations, Gemini and Perplexity sometimes followed my intentions more closely than ChatGPT. However, occasional prompt adherence is different from precise control. I could request changes, but I could not reliably preserve the successful parts of one image while changing only the unsuccessful parts. Which produced the most unexpected results?
Perplexity produced one of the clearest unexpected errors through the miner’s arm and hand anatomy. Gemini’s enlarged wheel was another surprising departure. Across the experiment, the human-like superhero-miner and the prominence of the background crowds were the most unexpected developments. Some surprises offered new visual possibilities, but others distracted from the composition. Which required the most prompting?
All three required repeated corrections. ChatGPT felt like it needed more redirection because I found its instruction-following less consistent. However, I used the same broad sequence of iterations across the tools, so my documentation does not establish that one objectively required more prompts than the others. Which interpreted language most literally?
There was no clear winner. All three appeared literal when they generated a standing, active miner from my written description, despite my intention to reference a seated figure. Their responses to “make the subject a miner” also suggest that they prioritized that immediate instruction over preserving the earlier combination. They followed individual directions more successfully than the larger conceptual relationship. Which seemed to rely most heavily on familiar visual conventions?
ChatGPT and Gemini leaned more strongly toward cinematic, modern-looking imagery during the meme stage. Perplexity retained more of the sepia photographic atmosphere, but that could also become a convention: an “old photograph” appearance standing in for the original work’s particular lighting, space, and psychological tension. Where did outputs begin to feel generic?
They began to feel generic when the solitary worker became a dramatic mining character and the meme became a realistic superhero scene. The images retained recognizable subjects but lost some of the specificity that mattered to me: the overwhelming rock wall, the confined space, the ambiguous darkness, and the feeling of a doorway leading nowhere. Did one tool encourage particular aesthetics or kinds of ideas?
In this experiment, Perplexity encouraged an aged, sepia photographic direction, while ChatGPT and Gemini moved toward a more cinematic interpretation during the collage stage. Those tendencies influenced what I had to correct. Instead of only developing my idea, I was also working against each tool’s apparent stylistic direction.
Did changing your prompt substantially change the result or mostly produce variations of the same thing?
Changing the prompt sometimes substantially changed the subject, but not necessarily in a useful cumulative way. Introducing the meme removed the miner. Restoring the miner brought the images closer to the photograph but weakened the collage. The changes felt more like switching between interpretations than gradually building one coherent image. This led me to explicitly ask for the miner and superhero to become the same character. What could you accomplish more effectively yourself?
I could more effectively control composition, preserve the original’s negative space, and decide exactly how the photograph and meme should interact. Through drawing or manual collage, I could keep the seated posture, place the buttons deliberately, and determine whether the relationship should feel humorous, oppressive, ambiguous, or unsettling. I could make those decisions without repeatedly risking the loss of elements I wanted to retain. What did the AI help you see or consider that you might not have generated otherwise?
The unexpected superhero-miner helped me consider combining the two identities within one figure rather than placing separate images beside each other. The crowds also introduced the possibility of social pressure: the character’s dilemma could be witnessed rather than experienced alone. Most importantly, the process clarified what I valued in the original photograph. Its power, for me, came less from the general subject of mining than from stillness, darkness, confinement, and human vulnerability. The experiment helped me articulate those priorities, even though I did not feel the outputs surpassed my expectations or became fully my own work.
